# Dogs' Paws: Anatomy, Common Problems, and Care Tips

A dog's paws support movement, help with traction, cushion each step, and protect sensitive tissues from rough ground. Healthy paws also support comfort during walking, running, and play.

## Basic anatomy of a dog's paw

A dog's paw includes several parts that work together to support balance, movement, and protection.

- **Paw pads:** These thick, cushioned surfaces help absorb impact and protect the foot from rough ground.
- **Digital pads or toe pads:** These smaller pads sit under each toe and help with balance, traction, and shock absorption.
- **Metacarpal or metatarsal pad:** This larger central pad sits behind the toe pads and helps support weight and stability.
- **Claws or nails:** Dogs use their nails for traction, digging, and gripping the ground during movement.
- **Dewclaw:** This higher toe on the inside of the leg does not always touch the ground, but it can help with grip and stability in some movements.
- **Toes:** The toes spread and flex to help a dog balance, change direction, and move across uneven surfaces.
- **Skin and fur between the toes:** This area helps protect the spaces between the digits, but it can also trap moisture, debris, or irritants.

## What each part does

- **Paw pads** cushion the foot, absorb shock, and protect against rough surfaces.
- **Digital pads** support each toe and help maintain traction and balance.
- **Metacarpal or metatarsal pad** carries weight and helps steady the paw during standing and walking.
- **Claws or nails** help a dog grip the ground, dig, and gain extra stability.
- **Dewclaw** can assist with grip, especially during turning or holding objects.
- **Toes** help with balance, stepping, and adjusting to uneven terrain.
- **Skin and fur between the toes** shield delicate skin, though this area needs regular checks because debris and moisture can collect there.

## Common paw problems

- **Cuts and abrasions:** A dog may show licking, bleeding, tenderness, or reluctance to bear weight. Even small wounds matter because dirt and repeated pressure can slow healing.
- **Cracked or dry pads:** Pads may look rough, flaky, or split, and a dog may pull the paw away when touched. Cracks matter because they can become painful and may deepen over time.
- **Foreign objects between the toes:** Grass seeds, burrs, small stones, or other debris can cause sudden licking, chewing, or limping. These problems matter because trapped material can irritate the skin or lead to infection.
- **Overgrown or broken nails:** Nails may click loudly on hard floors, curl, snag, or bleed if torn. Nail problems matter because they can change how a dog walks and can cause pain.
- **Burns from hot pavement:** Paw pads may appear red or sensitive, and a dog may refuse to walk or lift the feet often. Burns matter because heat can damage the pads quickly.
- **Irritation from ice or salt:** A dog may lick the paws after winter walks, hold up the feet, or show redness between the toes. This matters because salt, ice, and cold surfaces can irritate the skin.
- **Allergies or infections:** Repeated licking, redness, odor, swelling, or discharge can appear around the toes or nail beds. These signs matter because ongoing irritation can worsen and may need veterinary attention.
- **Limping or swelling:** A dog may favor one paw, avoid activity, or react when the foot is touched. This matters because swelling or limping can point to a sprain, sting, foreign object, or other injury.

## Practical paw-care tips

- Inspect the paws regularly for cuts, swelling, redness, debris, and changes in the nails or pads.
- Trim nails carefully and in small amounts to avoid causing pain or bleeding.
- Clean and dry the paws after walks, especially after mud, snow, ice, or wet grass.
- Check pavement temperature before walks during hot weather to help prevent burns.
- Use pet safe moisturizers only when needed for dry pads, and avoid products made for people unless a veterinarian recommends them.
- Trim excess fur around the paws when appropriate so dirt, ice, and debris do not collect as easily.
- Contact a veterinarian if signs seem severe, last more than a short time, involve bleeding, or cause limping.

Regular paw checks can help catch small problems before they become more serious. Prompt veterinary care can help protect comfort and movement when something seems wrong.
